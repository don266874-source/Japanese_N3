Bite Size Japanese Podcast (Intermediate) - Ep10: 私の2022年の5つの目標

Felix's Learner Note:

Episode 10 is Layla at her most structured and forward-looking. She lays out five concrete goals for 2022: (1) earn more money through Patreon and creative work, (2) design the interior of a 200-year-old warehouse in Tomonoura, (3) grow her YouTube from 7,500 to 30,000-50,000 subscribers, (4) read more books to balance input and output, and (5) exercise daily with 50 sit-ups and 50 squats every morning. The grammar is deliberately simple because she is making promises to herself and to you — and the language is exactly what you need to make your own promises in Japanese.

What makes this episode a grammar goldmine is the density of 〜たい / 〜たくない / 〜たかった structures. Layla uses たい 14 times in 14 minutes — "お金を稼げるようになりたい" (I want to be able to earn money), "デザインの勉強も頑張りたい" (I want to work hard at studying design), "YouTubeをもっと頑張りたい" (I want to work harder at YouTube), "本をもっと読みたい" (I want to read more books), "運動したい" (I want to exercise). Every sentence is a self-contained goal statement you can steal for your own planning. The episode also models 〜ために (in order to) for connecting goals to actions, 〜ようにしたい (want to become able to) for ability goals, and 〜てしまう for admitting past failures (散歩をやめてしまいました = I ended up quitting walking).

For self-learners who struggle with "I want to" vs. "I want to be able to" in Japanese, this episode is the definitive contrast. 〜たい = "I want to do X"; 〜(よ)うになりたい = "I want to become able to do X"; 〜(よ)うにしたい = "I want to make it so that X happens." Layla moves between all three with zero hesitation, and the episode is the best single resource for internalizing the difference.

📚 Grammar Breakdown

1. 〜（よ）うになりたい（能力の願望）
Meaning: "I want to become able to ~." Verb volitional form (う/よう) + になりたい — expresses the desire to gain an ability, not just to do something.
In Text: お金を稼げるようになりたい (I want to become able to earn money); 本当にできるようになりたいです (I really want to become able to do it); サポートしてもらえるように (so that I can be supported); 続けられるようにしたいです (I want to make it so I can continue).
Note: N4. The difference between 〜たい and 〜(よ)うになりたい is crucial: 〜たい = "I want to do X" (the action itself); 〜(よ)うになりたい = "I want to become able to do X" (the ability). Self-learners often use 〜たい when they mean 〜(よ)うになりたい, creating sentences that sound like "I want to earn money" when they mean "I want to be in a position where I can earn money." Shadow the 「Verb potential form + ようになりたい」 chain as an "ability goal" template.

2. 〜たい（希望・願望）
Meaning: "Want to do / would like to." Verb stem (remove る/ます) + たい — expresses speaker's desire.
In Text: 成長したい (I want to grow); 頑張りたい (I want to work hard); デザインの勉強も頑張りたい (I want to work hard at studying design); YouTubeをもっと頑張りたい (I want to work harder at YouTube); 本をもっと読みたい (I want to read more books); 運動したい (I want to exercise); 体を良くしたい (I want to make my body better).
Note: N4. たい is the most basic desire-marker in Japanese. The common error is using it for other people's desires ("彼は食べたい" can only mean "I think he wants to eat" if you're inside his head — otherwise use たがっている). Layla stacks たい multiple times in a row to build a portrait of desired future self. The stacking is the grammar move to imitate. Also note the 〜たいと思います softener: シェアしたいと思います — it makes desire sound less demanding.

3. 〜てしまう（不可逆・残念な結果）
Meaning: "End up doing ~ / did ~ (with a sting of regret or accident)." Verb て-form + しまう — marks completion, often with unwanted consequences.
In Text: ストップしてしまいました (I ended up stopping); 散歩をやめてしまいました (I ended up quitting walking); 本を読むのを止めてしまいました (I ended up stopping reading books); 気づかずに続けてしまいます (I end up continuing without noticing).
Note: N4. 〜てしまう is the emotional core of this episode's "failure" narrative. Layla uses it to describe habits she quit (walking, reading) and the risk of continuing wrong methods without realizing. The nuance is "I did it, and now it's done, and I'd rather not have." Self-learners learn てしまう from textbooks as "doing something completely" (e.g., 食べてしまいました = "ate it all up") but the dominant use in this episode is the "unwanted result" frame. Shadow the 「Verb て-form + しまいました / しまって」 chain as a "past regret" template.

4. 〜ために（目的・原因）
Meaning: "For the purpose of / in order to / because of." Noun + のために or Verb dictionary form + ために — marks a goal or cause.
In Text: そのためにも (for that too); 自分の生活をよくするために (to make one's life better); 仕事が効率よくできるようにするために (to be able to do work efficiently); 正しい日本語を覚えるために (to remember correct Japanese); 将来のスピーキングの練習になります (it becomes future speaking practice).
Note: N4. ために is the single most important purpose/cause frame in Japanese. The difference between ために (purpose) and から (because) is that ために looks forward to the goal while から looks backward at the reason. Self-learners often overuse から and underuse ために — the result sounds like giving excuses rather than stating intentions. Shadow the 「Verb dict. form + ために + action」 pattern; it's the standard "I did X in order to Y" frame.

5. 〜ようにする / 〜ようにしたい（状態の意図的変化）
Meaning: "Make it so that / try to / want to make it so that." Verb dictionary form + ようにする — marks an intentional change in state or habit. ようにしたい = "want to make it so that."
In Text: サポートしてもらえるように (so that I can be supported); 日本語の勉強がサポートできるように (so that I can support Japanese study); 長く楽にyoutubeを続けられるようにしたいです (I want to make it so I can continue YouTube long and easily); 正しくインプットしないと、間違ってアウトプットしたら大変です (if I don't input correctly, if I output incorrectly, it's a problem).
Note: N4. ようにする is the "habit change" grammar — it marks a deliberate effort to change a state or behavior. The difference between ようにする and ことにする is that ことにする = "decide to do (one time)" while ようにする = "make it a habit to do / try to do." Self-learners often confuse the two. Shadow the 「Verb potential form + ようにしたい」 chain as a "habit change goal" template.
