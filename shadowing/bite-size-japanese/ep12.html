Bite Size Japanese Podcast (Intermediate) - Ep12: How I Became 5 Times More Productive

Felix's Learner Note:

Every productivity podcast tells you to "just focus" or "wake up at 5am." This one is different. Layla — the voice behind Bite size Japanese — doesn't sell you a system. She holds up a mirror. One year ago, she was clocking 8 to 10 hours a day editing Instagram videos… and producing one 30-second clip. Today, she runs a podcast, a Patreon, a YouTube channel, short-form video, and weekly Zoom lessons — in less time. She says she's 5 times faster. Maybe 10.

What changed? Not a miracle app. Not a cooler desk. Two old enemies: dawdling (ダラダラ) and research-as-procrastination (調べる時間). She used to fall down rabbit holes learning camera gear, mic technique, editing software, what other teachers were doing — hours vanishing before a single frame got cut. She was a worrier (心配性), and worry ate her days.

Her fix? A business coach. A time-management coach. An editor for podcast subtitles. An editor for YouTube. A morning to-do list with a timer. And — surprisingly — morning cleaning. She wipes down her desk and kitchen before anything else, and says her concentration shifted overnight. This episode is shadowing gold: simple, repetitive, self-deprecating spoken Japanese about the thing every self-learner struggles with — turning effort into output.

📚 Grammar Breakdown

1. ～くらい / ～ぐらい
Meaning: Approximately / about / to the extent of.
In Text: 5倍くらい (about 5 times), 10倍くらい早く (maybe 10 times faster), 10時間くらい毎日 (roughly 10 hours a day).
Note: くらい is the casual spoken form of 約 / 程度. It follows nouns, verbs, and even whole clauses. N5 books teach it as "about a quantity," but in natural speech it softens every claim — くらい makes the speaker sound less definitive and more approachable. Layla uses it to hedge her productivity claims: she's not saying "exactly 10x," she's saying "ballpark, maybe even 10x." That hedging is native-level pragmatics.
2. ～に比べて / ～と比べて
Meaning: Compared to... / In comparison with...
In Text: 1年前に比べて (compared to a year ago), 1年前に私がしていたことと比えてみると (if I compare what I was doing a year ago with what I do now).
Note: 比べる is a regular ichidan verb. The て-form + みる pattern (比べてみると) means "try comparing" — it invites the listener into a thought experiment. N4 grammar, but it's a discourse anchor: every time Layla uses 比べて, she signals a before-and-after narrative beat. For shadowing, this is a high-frequency rhetorical pattern in podcast monologues.
3. ～すぎる / ～すぎ
Meaning: Too much / Excessively.
In Text: ここまであんなに迷わなくても良かった (I didn't need to worry this much), implied excess in 迷う時間がすごく長かった (the time I spent lost was absurdly long).
Note: While すぎる doesn't appear in its dictionary form here, the concept of excess threads the whole episode. When すぎる does attach to verbs (迷いすぎ), it means "way too much." Layla's self-critique is a masterclass in self-directed excess grammar. N4 pattern: stem form of verb/adjective + すぎる.
4. ～ようにする / ～ようになった
Meaning: Make it a habit to... / Have come to...
In Text: 時間を大切にしたいと思うようになりました (I've come to want to treasure my time), できるようになりました (became able to), 集中できるようになりました (became able to concentrate).
Note: The ～ようになる pattern describes a change in state or ability over time. It's not "I decided" (ことにした), it's "I've reached the point where…" Layla uses this to frame personal growth as an organic shift, not a forced decision. N4 grammar, essential for self-reflection narratives.
5. ～ため (目的) / ～ために
Meaning: For the purpose of / In order to.
In Text: 時間を上手に使うために必要だと思います (I think it's necessary in order to use time well).
Note: This is the purpose ため (not the cause ため). Layla uses it to justify hiring coaches and editors — not "because I'm lazy" but "in order to use time well, I need to delegate what I can't do." This reframe is important: ため turns a personal limitation into a strategic decision. N4 grammar, but the reasoning pattern is what makes it useful.
