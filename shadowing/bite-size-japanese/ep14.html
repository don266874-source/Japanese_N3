Bite Size Japanese Podcast (Intermediate) - Ep14: タピオカ作りにハマった

Felix's Learner Note:

Episode 14 of Bite Size Japanese opens with a simple question from host Layla (レイラ): what food have you been into lately? In Japanese, that is ハマっている (hamatte iru), from the verb ハマる — to get hooked on something. Her answer is two words: タピオカとチアシード — tapioca and chia seeds. From there the whole episode becomes a warm, honest story about how a self-declared trend-hater ended up making a fresh batch of tapioca every single day.

Tapioca was a massive boom in Japan — and around the world — about one or two years ago. But Layla admits she does not like chasing trends (トレンドを追いかけるのが好きじゃない), so during the entire bubble-tea craze she never once bought a drink. Then one day at the supermarket she spotted a cheap milk-tea tapioca for about 200 yen (200円くらいのミルクティーのタピオカ). She tried it, loved it, and fell for the black, chewy tapioca (黒いやつ、チューイーな) in particular. Because store-bought is expensive, she ordered a full kilogram on Amazon and now makes it at home. The method she walks through is wonderfully simple: bring a pot of water to a boil (水を沸騰させる), add the dried tapioca, simmer for about 20 minutes (20分くらい煮ます), then rinse it in cold water (冷たい水で洗います). She drinks it with soy milk (豆乳) in flavors like banana, black tea, malt, and matcha.

Halfway through, the topic turns playful and practical. Soy milk is healthy, she notes, but hers is quite sweet — and tapioca, apparently, is not very healthy either (あんまりヘルシーじゃないらしいです), more like potato powder than a vegetable. She then asks listeners to vote in the comments: sweet tapioca or not-sweet? Finally she shares yesterday's kitchen disaster — she made too much, put it in the fridge, and by morning the tapioca had soaked up all the water, gone soft, and turned brown instead of the deep black it should be. For shadowing practice this episode is full of everyday cooking and food talk. You will drill 〜にはまっている (to be hooked on ~), the reason marker 〜ので (because), the conditional 〜たら (when / if), the hearsay 〜らしい (apparently), and the resemblance pattern 〜みたいな／〜のような (like ~). By the end you can describe your own food obsession, your own recipe, and your own cooking mistakes — entirely in natural, bite-size Japanese.

📚 Grammar Breakdown

1. ～に比べて / ～より
Meaning: Compared to... / Than...
In Text: 広島にとても近いです (it's very close to Hiroshima).
Note: ～に比べて is the formal comparison pattern, while ～より is the casual particle. The key difference: に比べて frames the comparison as a perspective ("viewed relative to X"), while より simply marks the baseline ("more than X"). Both are N4, but に比べて is the one you'll hear in podcasts and presentations.
2. ～すぎる / ～すぎ
Meaning: Too much / Excessively.
In Text: めっちゃ可愛い (incredibly cute — colloquial intensifier), 千匹ぐらい (about a thousand).
Note: すぎる attaches to verb stems and adjectives to mean "too much." めっちゃ is a colloquial intensifier meaning "insanely/really" — a more casual version of とても. Layla uses it throughout this episode to describe the rabbits: めっちゃ可愛い, むっちゃ綺麗. It's casual spoken Japanese at its most natural.
3. ～ことにします / ～ことにする
Meaning: Decide to... / Make it a rule to...
In Text: 冬休みを2週間に長くしました (I made the winter break longer).
Note: ～ことにします is the grammar of active choice. N4 learners often confuse it with ～ようになる (organic change). Layla's にしました is the speaker taking control: not "it became two weeks" but "I made it two weeks." The difference is agency. When you use ことにします, you're the one driving the decision.
4. ～ため (目的・原因)
Meaning: For the purpose of / Because of.
In Text: 毒は人を殺すために作っていたので (the poison was made in order to kill people).
Note: The purpose ため (ために) appears in 人を殺すために — "in order to kill people." This is one of the heaviest uses of ため in the whole series: Layla uses it to explain the original purpose of the factory. When you say ために after a verb, you're stating intent — and here, the intent is grim. It's a useful pattern for historical narration: ～ために建てられた (built for the purpose of), ～のために使われた (was used for).
5. ～てくる / ～ていく (方向性・変化)
Meaning: Come to... / Go on to... / Begin to...
In Text: 戦争が終わった時 (when the war ended), 日本が戦争に負けたとき (when Japan lost the war), 日本に入ってきました (entered Japan).
Note: The ～てくる pattern appears in 入ってきました — "came in" (movement toward the speaker's perspective). Combined with とき (when/around the time), Layla creates a timeline of historical events. ～とき is N5 grammar, but its discourse function is N2: it structures narratives. For shadowing, listen for how とき doesn't mean "at the exact moment of" but "in the context of / around the time of." That vagueness is natural Japanese.
