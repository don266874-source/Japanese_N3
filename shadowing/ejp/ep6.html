Easy Japanese Podcast - Ep6: 今日何食べた？

Felix's Learner Note:

Easy Japanese episode 6 breaks its usual format: for the first time, the host welcomes a guest — Eiichiro Nagaya, a fellow podcaster from Gifu Prefecture, right in the middle of Japan. The topic is the most universal small talk there is: 今日何食べた？ (What did you eat today?). The guest had a soy-patty hamburger his mother bought him; the host cooked himself the chicken version of 酢豚 (subuta) — sweet and sour chicken — and comparing the two dinners kicks off a conversation packed with beginner vocabulary.

From there the episode becomes a learner's goldmine. You'll collect the core food adjectives — 甘い (sweet), 苦い (bitter), 辛い (spicy) — and untangle one of the trickiest false friends for English speakers: Japanese splits "hot" into 暑い／熱い (temperature) and 辛い (spicy), while English uses one word for all of it (plus "hot" as in attractive). The hosts then dig into katakana loanwords: why bread is パン (not from English "bread"), why パスタ means spaghetti in Japan, and why canned tuna is called シーチキン ("sea chicken") even though ツナ is the adopted English word — plus a detour into ブイヨン (bouillon, from French) and 香辛料 vs. 調味料.

The episode closes with study advice every learner should hear: anime Japanese (Naruto's だってばよ, for example) isn't how real people talk, but Ghibli films use natural everyday dialogue — watch those instead, and let repetition do its work. For shadowing, this episode is ideal: two voices trading turns, natural fillers and reactions like たしかに and なるほどね, and the definitional pattern 〜っていうのは repeated dozens of times.

1. 〜っていうのは（定義・話題提示）
Meaning: "X" means... / Speaking of X (topic + definition)
In Text: 回ったところっていうのは…意味ですね / 兼ねてっていうのは…みたいな感じですね / おかかっていうのは鰹節なんですけど.
Note: This is the episode's signature pattern — the hosts define almost every new word with 〜っていうのは, the casual spoken form of 〜というのは. It flags "here comes an explanation," making it the single most useful listening cue in natural Japanese podcasts. Shadow every instance; it trains you to both ask for and give definitions conversationally. (N4)
2. 〜とか（例示）
Meaning: ...and so on / such as (casual listing)
In Text: 可愛いとか、美味しいとか、あったかいとか / ハーブとか、ブラックペッパーとか / 昆布とか、おかかとか.
Note: とか lets you name examples without committing to a complete list — the casual counterpart of や and softer than a bare と. The hosts stack it constantly while rattling off adjectives and foods, so this episode doubles as an とか drill. It's often doubled (AとかBとか) for a natural rhythm. (N5)
3. 〜たところ（ちょうど今〜したばかり）
Meaning: Just (did) / have just now...
In Text: 今7時を回ったところです (it's just past seven now).
Note: Verb (past) + ところ pins an action to the exact moment of completion — "just barely past." The host uses it in the opening time check, and then immediately defines it with っていうのは, giving you a two-for-one grammar demo. Compare 〜ているところ (in the middle of) and 〜るところ (about to). (N4)
4. 〜がち（傾向・マイナス評価）
Meaning: Tends to... / is liable to... (often negative)
In Text: 英語は「暑い」って言われがちなんですけど (English tends to get used for "hot"...).
Note: Attach がち to a verb stem or noun to describe a recurring tendency — usually with a slightly critical nuance, like 忘れがち (forgetful) or 太りがち (prone to gaining weight). Here it nails how English speakers habitually default to one word for "hot." A high-frequency N3 pattern that instantly makes your speech sound native. (N3)
5. 〜を兼ねて（兼用）
Meaning: Also serving as / doubling as
In Text: 練習も兼ねてやってみようかなと思うので (we'll try it while also using it as practice).
Note: Noun + を兼ねて expresses doing one thing that simultaneously fulfills another purpose — one action, two birds. The host coins it here and then immediately defines it (兼ねてっていうのは練習も一緒にっていう意味ですね), a perfect natural-comprehension demo. Common in phrases like 買い物を兼ねて散歩する. (N3)
