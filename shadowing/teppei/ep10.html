Nihongo con Teppei (Beginner) - Ep10: お酒は好きですか？

Felix's Learner Note:

Episode 10 of Nihongo con Teppei opens with the usual cozy weather report — it's a rainy Friday evening in Tokyo, around 6 p.m., and Teppei notes the day is nearly done. He uses that to pivot into the episode's question: お酒は好きですか (do you like alcohol?).

The heart of the episode is a mini-lesson on Japanese drinking vocabulary. Teppei carefully untangles a word that confuses learners: お酒 (o-sake) can mean "alcohol" in general, while 日本酒 (nihonshu) means the specific rice brew — and he points out the word has these two meanings. He rolls through the menu: beer (his favorite), shochu, nihonshu, wine, tequila, vodka, whiskey, and models the 〜とか (such as) listing pattern. He's honest that he drinks about five times a week, then flips into a responsible note: 飲み過ぎ (drinking too much) is bad, so aim for ほどほど (moderation).

For shadowing, this episode is a clean drill on the は-topic question (お酒は好きですか), the とか example particle, the extremely useful 〜過ぎる (too much), the permission frame 〜てもいい (it's okay not to), and the 〜と思います / 〜かな "I think / I wonder" hedges. If you want to talk about drinks, habits, and moderation in Japanese without sounding blunt, this episode hands you the whole phrasebook.

📚 Grammar Breakdown

1. ～過ぎる（過剰・程度の超過）
Meaning: Too much ~ / Over-~ (excess)
In Text: 飲み過ぎ (drinking too much), 飲み過ぎは良くない (drinking too much isn't good), 飲み過ぎること (the act of over-drinking).
Note: 過ぎる attaches to the stem of verbs (and some nouns/adjectives) to mean "excess." 飲む → 飲み + 過ぎる → 飲み過ぎる. Teppei hammers this point as a health warning — 飲み過ぎは良くないです — making it both a grammar lesson and a life tip. Shadow the long すぎ sound; it's a high-frequency word you'll hear about food, work, and sleep too.
2. ～てもいい（許容・OK）
Meaning: It's okay to ~ / You may ~
In Text: 別に飲まなくてもいいと思います (I think it's fine not to drink).
Note: 〜てもいい means "it's permissible." Negated, 〜なくてもいい means "it's okay NOT to" — exactly Teppei's reassurance that you don't have to drink. This is N5 and one of the most socially useful patterns in Japanese: it lets you grant or withhold permission politely. Here it pairs with と思います (I think) to soften the opinion.
3. ～と思います / ～かな（考え・意向のぼかし）
Meaning: I think ~ / I wonder ~ (hedged thought / intent)
In Text: ビールを飲もうかなと思います (I think I'll have a beer), この後も雨かな (I wonder if it'll rain after this too).
Note: 〜と思います wraps a statement as a personal opinion ("I think"), softening it. 〜かな is a soft "I wonder," often trailing off. Teppei uses both to keep his tone casual and non-authoritative — 飲もうかなと思います is the classic "I think I'll ~" for a loose plan. Shadow these to sound like you're thinking out loud, not declaring facts.
