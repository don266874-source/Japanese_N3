Nihongo con Teppei (Beginner) - Ep11: コーヒーは好きですか

Felix's Learner Note:

Episode 11 of Nihongo con Teppei picks up right where episode 10 left off — last time was alcohol, this time it's coffee. Teppei opens with his usual live check-in, sets the 4-minute timer, and immediately asks the listener the episode's driving question: コーヒーは好きですか、それとも好きじゃないですか (do you like coffee, or don't you)?

What follows is a fast, friendly tour through coffee vocabulary. He names the big ones — black coffee, café au lait, cappuccino, espresso, Nespresso, caramel macchiato — then pivots to a genuinely useful cultural point: in Japanese there are two words, カフェ (cafe) and 喫茶店 (kissaten), and Starbucks counts as the former, not the latter. He closes with a caffeine caution for people who get nervous from it, pointing them toward decaf and green tea. Every drink is another noun drilled inside the 〜について (about) and それとも (or) frames.

For shadowing, this is one of the most vocabulary-dense early episodes. You'll lock in 〜について (about), the それとも choice-question, the 〜というのは definition pattern (喫茶店というのは…), the caution pattern 〜ように気をつけてください (be careful so as not to), and the soft 〜かもしれない (might be). If you've ever stood frozen in a Japanese cafe unable to name your order, this episode hands you the whole menu.

📚 Grammar Breakdown

1. ～か、それとも～か（選択疑問）
Meaning: ~ or ~? (choice question)
In Text: ブラックコーヒーが好きですか、それともカフェオレが好きですか (do you like black coffee, or café au lait?), 好きですか、それとも好きじゃないですか.
Note: それとも is the dedicated "or" for joining two full questions — unlike か (which can also mean "or" inside a list), それとも always sits between two questions and signals a real either/or. Teppei stacks it all episode long, so it's a natural drill. Shadow the rising-then-falling intonation: question A ですか ↗、それとも question B ですか ↘.
2. ～というのは～です（定義・説明）
Meaning: ~ is defined as ~ / What we call ~ is ~ (definition / explanation)
In Text: 喫茶店というのは日本式、日本スタイルのカフェですね (Kissaten means Japanese-style cafe).
Note: というのは (often shortened to とは) introduces a definition or re-explanation of a term — it's the "that thing we call X is actually Y" pattern. Here Teppei uses it to explain the cultural distinction between カフェ and 喫茶店. It's N4 and hugely useful whenever you want to define a word for a listener. Model: [term] + というのは + [explanation] + です.
3. ～ように（目的・注意）／～ように気をつけてください
Meaning: So as to ~ / Be careful so that ~ (purpose / caution)
In Text: 飲み過ぎないように気をつけてください (please be careful not to drink too much).
Note: The ない-form + ように expresses a goal or precaution — "in such a way that X doesn't happen." 気をつけてください (please be careful) is the most common verb paired with it. Teppei uses it as a gentle health warning at the end. Compare: 忘れないように (so as not to forget) vs 忘れるように (so as to forget) — the negative is the caution reading. Shadow the whole set phrase as one unit.
5. ～かもしれない（推定・可能性）
Meaning: Might / May / Perhaps ~ (conjecture)
In Text: カフェインが苦手な人がいるかもしれないですね (there might be people who are bad with caffeine).
Note: かもしれない attaches to a plain form to soften a statement into "might be." Teppei uses it to hedge his assumption that some listeners can't handle caffeine — a polite, low-commitment way to generalize. Note the polite version かもしれないです (rather than the plainer かもしれません) fits his casual register. Great for shadowing softer opinions: 忙しいかもしれない (might be busy).
