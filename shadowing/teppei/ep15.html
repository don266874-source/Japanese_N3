Nihongo con Teppei (Beginner) - Ep15: 天気について

Felix's Learner Note:

Episode 15 of Nihongo con Teppei opens with Teppei doing his usual playful restart — he pretends the show is already ending, jokes about always saying "well then" (それでは), and checks in with the audience. He notes that today in Tokyo it's cloudy, and lately the weather has been bad. That small remark becomes the real topic of the episode: 天気について, about the weather — and specifically the question he gets asked again and again by his own students: 日本の天気はどうですか (how's the weather in Japan?).

The honest answer, he explains, is that it's a very hard question — because Japan is one long, slender country stretching from Hokkaido in the north to Okinawa in the south. Hokkaido gets winter cold comparable to Russia and enormous snowfall, while Okinawa stays warm enough that people get by with a thin coat and almost never need a real down jacket. Even on the main island of Honshu the north (Niigata, Fukushima, Akita) gets constant rain and snow, but Tokyo sees snow only about once a year, for a day or two. Then he turns to his own home, Kyushu: Fukuoka in the north and Kagoshima in the south are noticeably different, with Kagoshima staying fairly warm even in winter.

For shadowing practice this episode is gold for building your answer frame. You'll drill the topic marker 〜について, the reason phrase なぜかというと (if you ask why), the explanatory tone of 〜んですね, the degree comparison 〜と変わらないくらい (almost as ... as), and the reason-listing 〜し〜し. By the end you'll be able to answer 日本の天気はどうですか with real nuance — because, as Teppei keeps repeating, the honest answer is always "it depends on the place."

📚 Grammar Breakdown

3. 〜んです／〜んですね（説明・納得のムード）
Meaning: (Explanatory tone) It's that... / the thing is...
In Text: 日本の天気をよく聞かれるんですね (I often get asked...), 沖縄は暖かいんですよね (Okinawa is warm, you see), 本格的な寒さは来ないんです (real cold just doesn't come, you know).
Note: Adding んです (or んですね) to a sentence gives it an explanatory, "let me fill you in" flavor — it connects what you're saying to the listener's situation or your own reasoning. Spoken Japanese leans on it constantly. In weather talk it turns a bare fact into a natural explanation, which is exactly the register you want for real conversations.
4. 〜くらい／〜ぐらい（程度・おおよそ）
Meaning: About ~ / approximately / to the extent of ~
In Text: ロシアと変わらないくらい寒い (as cold as Russia, ~cold to the degree of not differing from Russia), 一年に一回くらい (about once a year), 薄いコートぐらい (about a thin coat).
Note: くらい/ぐらい marks approximate degree or quantity. Combined with 〜と変わらない (no different from) it becomes a vivid comparison: "cold to the point of being no different from Russia." This episode is a small goldmine of くらい/ぐらい for describing weather intensity and frequency — a must for N5 learners describing their own climate.
5. 〜し〜し（理由の並列）
Meaning: And ~, and ~ (listing reasons / circumstances)
In Text: 寒い土地もあるし、寒い日もあると思うんですけど (there are cold areas, and I think there are cold days, but...), 北の国は雨や雪が降るし (in the north it rains and snows, and...).
Note: Repeating 〜し after verbs or nouns stacks reasons or circumstances, implying "and there are more where that came from." It softens a claim and makes your explanation feel generous and natural. Teppei uses it to acknowledge that even "warm" Okinawa has cold spots — a great habit for balanced, native-sounding descriptions.
